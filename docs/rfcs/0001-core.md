# RFC 0001: SalahCore and SalahFixtures

- **Status:** Draft for 0.1.0. Both 0.1 consumers, `MosqueBoard` and `CalculatedPhone`, have reported: their findings are in §9 and §10. Open until shipped apps report (STANDARD.md, Releases).
- **Products:** SalahCore, SalahFixtures.
- **Started:** 2026-10-13.
- **Supersedes:** the API sketch in the SalahKit proposal, §7.1 and §7.6.

This RFC is the contract for SalahCore. The JSON vectors in `Vectors/` encode its behavior, and the implementation is tested against them. Section 5 lists every change from the proposal's sketch and why it was made. Section 10 records changes made after the vectors were reviewed; each one needs a written reason there.

References like "§7.1" point to the SalahKit proposal (`salahkit-public-first-proposal.md`).

---

## 1. Summary

SalahCore turns settings or published timetable data into a prayer **schedule**: typed events, each owned by a civil day, with explicit coverage. Apps ask the schedule bounded questions: a day, the events in a range, the state at a moment, or a refresh plan for a widget. The answers never invent missing times. A gap in the source becomes a gap in the answer.

Calculation is not part of SalahCore. A calculated source (SalahCalculation, RFC 0002) plugs in through `PrayerSource`, the same protocol `TimetableSource` implements for published times.

SalahFixtures supplies ready-made `TimetableSource` data for previews and tests.

## 2. Scope

In SalahCore 0.1:
- the domain types (`PrayerKind`, `CivilDay`, `PrayerEvent`, `PrayerDay`, `PrayerLocation`, `PrayerObservance`);
- the source contract (`PrayerSource`, `PrayerDayAvailability`, `DayCoverage`, `ScheduleGapReason`);
- published times (`TimetableSource`, `WallClockTime`, `TimetableTime`, `WallTimeResolution`) and `IqamaRules`;
- the schedule (`PrayerSchedule`, `ScheduleEvents`, `ScheduleCoverage`, `ScheduleGap`);
- state (`StatePolicy`, `ScheduleState`, `PrayerState`, `PrayerEventStatus`);
- refresh planning (`RefreshStrategy`, `RefreshPlan`, `RefreshPlanError`);
- words and numbers (`PrayerNames`, `PrayerDurationFormat`, `PrayerLocale`, `HijriDateFormat`).

Not in SalahCore: calculation (SalahCalculation), WidgetKit timelines (SalahWidgets), notifications (SalahAlerts), SwiftUI (SalahUI), location services, network fetching of timetables, and interface text other than prayer vocabulary.

## 3. Public API

Every public type is `nonisolated` and `Sendable`, so the API behaves the same whether a client module defaults to `MainActor` or `nonisolated` isolation. The package uses `swift-tools-version: 6.2`, the first version that accepts `nonisolated` on type declarations.

## 4. Behavior

### 4.1 Civil days and time zones

- A `CivilDay` is a calendar date. It becomes a span of time only together with a time zone: the location's zone, never the device's.
- `start(in: Z)` is local midnight of the day in `Z`, resolved as a wall time (§4.2) with `repeated: .earlier` and `nonexistent: .nextValid`. Where a zone skips midnight, the day starts at the first valid instant after it; where a zone skips a whole date (Pacific/Apia, 2011-12-30), that date's interval is empty.
- `interval(in: Z)` is `[start(D), start(D+1))`.
- `adding(days:)` and `days(to:)` are calendar arithmetic. Code never adds 86,400 seconds to move between days. Arithmetic on an invalid date (February 30) normalizes it the way the proleptic Gregorian day count does.
- `init(containing:in:)` is the Gregorian date of the instant in the zone.
- `weekday` follows `Calendar`'s numbering: 1 is Sunday, 6 is Friday.
- A `CivilDay` encodes as `"YYYY-MM-DD"` (four-digit year). Decoding rejects other shapes.

### 4.2 Wall times and their resolution

A `WallClockTime` names a local clock time on an owning day: `hour` 0-23, `minute` 0-59, `dayOffset` 0 or 1. With `dayOffset: 1`, the clock time is on the civil day after the owning day; that's how a timetable writes Monday's Isha at 00:15 on Tuesday. Other values are invalid data.

To resolve `(D, w)` in zone `Z`: let `L` be the local date-time `(D + w.dayOffset, w.hour, w.minute, 00)`. The candidates are every instant whose local time in `Z` equals `L` exactly.
- **One candidate:** that instant.
- **Two candidates** (a repeated time, when clocks fall back): `.earlier` takes the first, `.later` the second, `.reject` leaves the time unresolved.
- **No candidate** (a nonexistent time, when clocks spring forward): `.nextValid` takes the instant at which the skipped range ends, which is the first valid local time after `L`. It does not shift `L` by the size of the jump and keep its minutes: in a gap from 02:00 to 03:00, 02:30 resolves to 03:00. `.reject` leaves the time unresolved.

The resolver handles any offset change, not only one-hour daylight saving shifts.

The default policy rejects both cases. Unresolved prayer times make their kind missing with reason `.unresolvedWallTime` (§4.4). The same policy type governs iqama times.

`.instant` times are already resolved by their publisher and are never reinterpreted.

`WallClockTime` encodes as `"HH:MM"` with an optional `"+1"` suffix (`"00:15+1"`). Hours may be written with one digit when decoding (`"5:05"`), and always encode with two. Decoding checks the shape only (one or two hour digits, a colon, two minute digits, optionally `+` and digits), so `"24:00"`, `"15:60"` and `"00:15+2"` decode, and the row checks of §4.4 make their day invalid. That includes a day offset with more digits than `Int` holds.

### 4.3 The `PrayerSource` contract

A source answers one question: what are the events of owning day `D` at this location? `PrayerSchedule` enforces the rules below on every source, including custom ones, and turns a violation into `.unavailable(.invalidData)` for that day. A calculated source (B1's SalahCalculation) must meet all of them.

1. **Purity.** For the same day and location, a source returns the same answer. It doesn't block on I/O; fetching new data is the app's job. It may be called concurrently and more than once per day.
2. **Expected kinds and lead.** `expectedKinds` and `maximumLead` are fixed for the source's lifetime, and `expectedKinds` isn't empty. A source with no expected kinds makes every day `.unavailable(.invalidData)`. The schedule reads the lead as `lead = min(max(maximumLead, 0), 12 hours)`. A source that wraps another one passes both through.
3. **Shape of an available day.** For `.available(day, coverage)`:
   - `day.day` is the requested day, and the requested day is a valid date;
   - `day.timeZone` is the location's time zone (the same identifier);
   - every event's `scheduleDay` is the requested day;
   - every event's kind is in `expectedKinds`, and no kind appears twice;
   - there is at least one event.
4. **Owning days and displacement.** Every event of owning day `D` occurs in `[start(D) − lead, start(D+2))` in the location's zone: on its own civil day or the next one, and before its owning day only within the source's lead. Monday's Isha at 00:15 on Tuesday belongs to Monday. A published time is never before its day (`TimetableSource` declares no lead); a calculated Fajr can be, where the clock runs behind the Sun: Kazan's Fajr under the middle-of-the-night rule falls between 23:40 and 23:52 on the evening before, from May to August (RFC 0002 §4.4). An event outside that span is invalid data, never moved to another day.
5. **Chronology within a day.** Events are ordered by instant, and their instants strictly increase in canonical kind order: fajr < sunrise < dhuhr < asr < maghrib < isha, over the kinds present. A violation is invalid data. A source never reorders or renames events to repair it.
6. **Chronology across days.** No event of day `D` occurs after an event of a later day. Equal instants are allowed (a middle-of-the-night high-latitude rule can make Isha and the next Fajr coincide). Without a lead, rules 4 and 6 for consecutive days imply the rest; with one, an event late on `D+1` that belongs to `D` and an early event of `D+2` can cross when `D+1` has no events. A source that can't meet this should report the affected kinds as missing. When the schedule finds a violation between an available day and the next day that has events, it keeps both days and reports the overlap as a gap (§4.6).
7. **Coverage.** `.complete` means every expected kind is present. `.partial(missing:)` names exactly the expected kinds that are absent, each with its reason; at least one event is present. A day with no events is `.unavailable(reason)`. Iqama never affects coverage.
8. **Unavailability.** `.noPublishedData` when the source has nothing for the day or kind; `.calculationUnavailable` when a calculation can't produce it (for example, Isha in a polar summer); `.unresolvedWallTime` when every time failed resolution; `.invalidData` when the data contradicts this contract.
9. **No substitution.** A source never fills a day or kind from another day, a neighbouring location, or a computed fallback unless the app explicitly built such a source. Old values never stand in for a missing day.
10. **Iqama.** An event's `iqama`, when present, satisfies `time <= iqama < start(D+2)`. The schedule discards (sets to `nil`) an iqama outside that span instead of invalidating the day, because prayer-time coverage doesn't depend on it.
11. **Observance.** `.jumuah` appears only on a `.dhuhr` event of a Friday (`weekday == 6`), and only when the source declares it. A source never infers it from the weekday. A violation is invalid data.

### 4.4 `TimetableSource`

`TimetableSource` holds published rows and resolves them in the location's time zone when asked. Its initializer doesn't throw: a bad row affects only its own day, so a yearly table with one typo still serves the other 364 days.

**Row checks** (at initialization; a failing row makes its day `.unavailable(.invalidData)`):
- the row's day is a valid date, and no other row has the same day (every duplicate is invalid);
- every kind in `times` is in `expectedKinds`;
- every kind in `iqama` is a prayer (not sunrise) that has a time in the row;
- every kind in `observances` has a time in the row, and `.jumuah` appears only on dhuhr of a Friday;
- every `WallClockTime` has an hour 0-23, a minute 0-59 and a day offset of 0 or 1.

**`prayerDay(D, at: location)`:**
1. `D` invalid, or `expectedKinds` empty: `.unavailable(.invalidData)`.
2. No row for `D`, or one row with no times: `.unavailable(.noPublishedData)`. Two or more rows for `D` are invalid (step 3) even when one of them has no times.
3. The row failed its checks: `.unavailable(.invalidData)`.
4. Resolve each time in `location.timeZone` with `wallTimeResolution` (§4.2). An unresolved time leaves its kind missing with reason `.unresolvedWallTime`.
5. Every resolved time must lie in `[start(D), start(D+2))`, and the resolved times must strictly increase in canonical kind order. Otherwise: `.unavailable(.invalidData)`.
6. Resolve iqama the same way. An unresolved iqama, or one outside `[time, start(D+2))`, becomes `nil`.
7. Copy the row's observances onto the events (default `.ordinary`).
8. If no time resolved: `.unavailable(.unresolvedWallTime)`. Otherwise the day is available; its coverage lists each missing expected kind with `.noPublishedData` (not in the row) or `.unresolvedWallTime`.

```json
      "iqama": { "fajr": "06:00", "dhuhr": "13:30" },
      "observances": { "dhuhr": "jumuah" }
    },
    {
      "day": "2026-06-21",
      "times": { "fajr": "02:51", "dhuhr": "13:12", "asr": "17:31",

```

`wallTimeResolution`, `iqama` and `observances` are optional (defaults: reject/reject, none, none). A time is a wall-time string or an ISO 8601 instant with `Z` or an offset (`"2026-06-21T23:05:00+01:00"`). Decoding fails only for malformed JSON values; checkable content errors become invalid days as above.

### 4.5 Iqama rules

`PrayerSchedule(source:location:iqama:)` applies `IqamaRules` to events whose `iqama` is still `nil` after the source. A source's published iqama always wins over a rule. A published iqama that the schedule discards (§4.3 rule 10) counts as none, so the rule applies.
- A `.jumuah` event uses only `rules.jumuah`. With no Jumu'ah rule it gets no iqama; the Dhuhr rule doesn't apply to Jumu'ah, whose congregation follows the khutbah.
- Other prayer events use `rules[kind]`. Rules for sunrise are ignored.
- `.after(minutes: m)` gives `time + m × 60 s`, counted in elapsed time, so it is unaffected by a clock change. A negative `m` gives no iqama.
- `.at(w)` resolves `w` on the event's owning day in the location's zone with `wallTimeResolution`. An unresolved time gives no iqama.
- The result must lie in `[time, start(D+2))`; otherwise the event gets no iqama.

### 4.6 Schedule queries and coverage

**`day(_:)`** asks the source, enforces §4.3, then applies iqama rules. **`day(containing:)`** is `day(CivilDay(containing: date, in: location.timeZone))`: the civil date, so at 00:10 on Tuesday it returns Tuesday's day, not Monday's overnight Isha.

**Context.** A query over `[a, b]` reads owning days `civil(a) − 2` through `civil(b + lead)`, where `civil(x)` is the civil day containing `x` in the location's zone. The two earlier days provide backward context for the state (yesterday's Isha) and bound unknown intervals. With a lead, the next day joins the context as soon as its earliest possible event could fall by `b`; without one, the last day is `civil(b)`.

**Events order.** Events are ordered by instant, then owning day, then canonical kind. This order breaks ties everywhere below.

**Missing-event windows.** Every expected kind `k` missing from a context day `D` (every expected kind, if `D` is unavailable) may occur anywhere in a window `[lo, hi)`:
- `lo` = the latest of `start(D) − lead`, the instants of `D`'s known events whose kind precedes `k`, and the instants of `D − 1`'s known events;
- `hi` = the earliest of `start(D + 2)`, the instants of `D`'s known events whose kind follows `k`, and the instants of `D + 1`'s known events.

A neighbor day outside the context adds no bound. A window with `lo >= hi` is empty and dropped. A date that a clock change skips entirely (an empty interval, §4.1) is still a context day: its events could only occur on the next date, so without data it has a window like any other day. Missing kinds of the same day with the same window and reason form one `ScheduleGap(interval: [lo, hi), day: D, kinds:, reason:)`; the reason is the kind's `DayCoverage.partial` reason, or the day's unavailability reason. Gaps of different reasons can share an interval.

**Overlap gaps.** When an available day `D` and `E`, the next context day that has events (`D+1` unless the days between have none), violate cross-day chronology (§4.3 rule 6), the schedule keeps both days' events and adds `ScheduleGap(interval: [first(E), last(D)), day: D, kinds: D's kinds whose events are later than first(E), reason: .invalidData)`, where `first` and `last` are the earliest and latest event instants of a day. These pairs are enough: when every pair is in order, so is every pair of days, and otherwise their gaps cover every interval between an event and an earlier event of a later day.

**Coverage of `[a, b]`.** `requested = [a, b]`. `gaps` are the context's gaps clipped to `[a, b)` (`[max(lo, a), min(hi, b))`), dropping empty ones, sorted by start, then day, then reason in declaration order (`noPublishedData`, `calculationUnavailable`, `invalidData`, `unresolvedWallTime`). `knownIntervals` is `[a, b]` minus the union of the clipped gaps, as maximal intervals; empty intervals are dropped. A zero-length request (`a = b`) keeps each gap with `lo <= a < hi` as the zero-length interval `[a, a]`, and has the one known interval `[a, a]` only when it keeps no gap. `isComplete` is true when there are no gaps.

**`events(from: a, through: b)`** returns the known events with `a <= time <= b`, in events order, and the coverage of `[a, b]`. Events of partial days are included; an overnight event appears once, in its owning day.

### 4.7 State

`state(at: t, through: b, policy:)` uses the known events `E` and the windows `W` of the context of `[t, b]`. `W` is every gap of the context as `[lo, hi)`, unclipped: the missing-event windows and the overlap gaps of §4.6, so the state asserts nothing across data that contradicts the contract. `W_p` is the windows whose kinds include a prayer (a window holding only sunrise isn't in it). Ties follow the events order.

- **`previous`:** the latest event `p` in `E` with `p.time <= t`, unless some window `w` in `W` has `w.lo <= t` and `w.hi > p.time`; then `nil`.
- **`latestPrayer`:** the same with prayer events and `W_p`.
- **`next`:** the earliest event `n` in `E` with `t < n.time <= b`, unless some window `w` in `W` has `w.lo < n.time` and `w.hi > t`; then `nil`. An event after `b` is never `next`.
- **`nextPrayer`:** the same with prayer events and `W_p`.
- **`justStarted`:** `latestPrayer`, while `t` is before the end of its window: `time + d` for `.fixed(d)`; for `.untilIqama(fallback: d)`, its iqama if it has one, else `time + d`. Otherwise `nil`.
- **`isApproaching`:** `nextPrayer != nil` and `t >= nextPrayer.time − policy.approaching`, where that instant is the same one `changesAt` uses as a candidate, so a duration that isn't exact in binary (0.1 s) still turns the flag on at its candidate.
- **Availability:** `.available` when any of `previous`, `latestPrayer`, `next` or `nextPrayer` is known; otherwise `.unavailable`. "No usable local state" means exactly that.
- **`coverage`:** the coverage of `[t, b]`.
- **`changesAt`:** the earliest instant `c` in `(t, b]` at which the semantic state differs from the one at `t`, else `nil`. The semantic state is the availability, the identities of the five event fields, `isApproaching`, and the set of unclipped gaps containing the instant (`lo <= c < hi`). It can only change at event instants, gap bounds, `nextPrayer.time − approaching` and just-started window ends, so those are the only candidates. The semantic state at a candidate `c` is that of `state(at: c, through: b)`: the context moves with the instant. Because a gap belongs to its owning day, an expired or empty timetable changes state at every midnight, when the days of the gaps containing the instant change; a page that names the missing day needs that update.

Policy durations below zero count as zero.

**Conventions this gives.** Every state holds on a half-open interval `[from, to)`: it starts at its change instant and lasts until the next one. In particular, the moment a window opens, the schedule stops asserting a predecessor: at the instant of Maghrib on a day whose Isha is missing, `previous` and `latestPrayer` are already `nil`, because Isha may follow at any moment. A known event that bounds a window from above becomes `previous` at its own instant.

**What this means for the cases in §7.1.**
- After Isha on the last published day, the state stays `.available` with `previous` and `latestPrayer` set and `next == nil`, `nextPrayer == nil`, until the next civil day starts.
- A missing middle day followed by a known day: the later day's Fajr never becomes `next` while the missing day's window lies before it.
- A partial row still lists its times through `day(_:)` and `events(from:through:)`, but no state field asserts a predecessor or successor across its missing event's window.
- `latestPrayer` is an occurrence, not a validity judgment: after sunrise, `previous` is sunrise and `latestPrayer` is still Fajr.

**`status(of: event)`** for a row in a list, comparing events by `id`:
- `.justStarted` if the event is `justStarted`;
- `.passed` if `event.time <= date`;
- `.next(approaching: event is nextPrayer && isApproaching)` if the event is `next`, or if `next` is `nil` and the event is `nextPrayer`;
- `.upcoming` otherwise.

### 4.8 Refresh planning

`refreshPlan(from: s, through: e, strategy:, policy:, limit:)` plans the dates where a widget needs a new entry, and the state for each.

**Errors**, checked in this order, the first that applies thrown: `e <= s`: `.nonPositiveHorizon`. `limit < 2`: `.limitTooSmall`. For `.steps`, `fine` or `coarse` under one second, or `window < 0`: `.invalidStrategy`. A step under a second can't change what a widget shows, and near today's dates it approaches what `Date` can tell apart, so a grid of them would never finish.

**Lookahead.** Every entry's state is `state(at: date, through: B, policy:)` with the fixed bound `B = start(civil(e) + 2)`. A widget near the end of its horizon therefore still knows the next prayer that falls just after the horizon.

**Transitions.** `c₀ = s`, and `cᵢ₊₁` is the `changesAt` of the state at `cᵢ`, stopping at `nil` or at the first value `>= e`. These are every instant in `(s, e)` where the known state or coverage changes, including a coverage boundary in a partial or expired timetable.

**Dates.**
- `.stateChanges`: `s` and the transitions.
- `.steps(fine:, coarse:, window:)`: `s`, the transitions, and
  - fine dates `p.time + k × fine` (any integer `k`, `|k × fine| <= window`) for every known prayer event `p` (not sunrise) of the schedule within `[s − window, e + window]`, read from that interval's context (§4.6) even where it's longer than one query's limit;
  - coarse dates `s + k × coarse` (`k >= 1`), except those within `window` of a known prayer's instant (`|date − p.time| <= window`);
  all restricted to `(s, e)`. A `window` longer than `maximumQueryDays` days counts as that long, so a plan never reads more than three query limits of days.

Dates are sorted and deduplicated.

**Budget.** The plan reserves one slot for its cutoff. If the dates fit (`count + 1 <= limit`), every date becomes an entry and `validUntil = e`. Otherwise the plan keeps the first `limit − 1` dates as entries and `validUntil` is the next date: the plan ends its validity at the first required change it can't hold, instead of skipping it and claiming continued coverage.

`validUntil` is where SalahWidgets places its timeline-expired entry. It's distinct from a source-data gap, which appears as a state inside the plan. These dates say when the content changes; WidgetKit still decides when it shows them.

### 4.9 Names, durations, locale and Hijri dates

**`PrayerNames`** reads the package's String Catalog, `Resources/Localizable.xcstrings`, with an explicit locale, whatever the process language is. Keys follow Salah's catalog: `prayer.<kind>`, `prayer.<kind>.abbr`, `prayer.dhuhr.jumuah` and `prayer.dhuhr.jumuah.abbr`. `name(of: event)` uses the Jumu'ah entry only when `event.observance == .jumuah`; a Friday alone never renames Dhuhr. Overrides replace catalog values per entry.

Language matching tries, in order: language-script-region, language-script, language-region, language, then the catalog's source language (English). The catalog ships as a copied resource and is read as JSON on every platform; where Xcode 26 compiles it anyway, the same entries come from the compiled tables (§6.1). Entries that a native speaker hasn't reviewed carry the catalog's `needs_review` state.

**`PrayerDurationFormat`** uses Foundation and rounds down everywhere. The value is `max(0, floor(seconds))`; non-finite input counts as zero.
- `.compact`: under a minute, narrow seconds (`45s`); under an hour, narrow minutes (`12m`); from an hour, `H:MM` with the locale's time separator (`2:15`, Indonesian `2.15`). `.elapsed` adds a leading `+`.
- `.spoken`: under a minute, wide seconds (`45 seconds`); otherwise wide hours and minutes of the value rounded down to the minute (`2 hours, 15 minutes`, never `16`). Spoken text never carries a sign; the app's sentence says "remaining" or "ago".
- Persian (`fa`): Foundation's narrow units fall back to Latin letters, so the compact seconds and minutes tiers use Salah's Persian abbreviations (`ث`, `د`) after the locale's digits.
- `bidiIsolated` wraps the result in U+2068 FIRST STRONG ISOLATE and U+2069 POP DIRECTIONAL ISOLATE.

**`PrayerLocale.resolved`** sets the numbering system a bare identifier loses: `ar` → `arab`, `ur` and `fa` → `arabext`, `bn` → `beng`. An identifier that already names one (`ar-MA-u-nu-latn`, `ar@numbers=latn`) keeps it. Other languages are unchanged. Platforms disagree about these defaults (on Linux, Swift 6.2 gives `ar` Arabic-Indic digits while Swift 6.4 gives Latin ones), which is why the function exists.

**`HijriDateFormat`** formats with `DateFormatter`, the chosen Islamic calendar (Umm al-Qura by default), the given time zone and locale, and a localized template. `dayOffset` moves the date by whole days in that calendar first, which is how apps apply a moon-sighting correction.

### 4.10 Query limits

- A query never searches the source indefinitely. It reads the context days of §4.6 and nothing else.
- One query covers at most `maximumQueryDays` (400) civil days. An end later than `start(civil(a) + 400)` is clamped to it, and `coverage.requested` (or `RefreshPlan.horizon`) shows the clamped interval.
- An end before the start counts as equal to the start.

## 5. Changes from the proposal's sketch

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Public types are `nonisolated`; `swift-tools-version` 6.2 | So the API stays nonisolated when a client compiles it with `MainActor` default isolation (EP §4.6). 6.2 is the first tools version that accepts the modifier on types; App Store builds already need Xcode 26. |
| `CivilDay` has `let` fields, a `"YYYY-MM-DD"` coding, arithmetic and zone helpers | A date value shouldn't change under its owner, and every consumer needs the same day math. A string is readable in JSON and portable to other languages. |
| `PrayerKind` is `Comparable` | Canonical order is used for chronology and sorting. |
| `DayCoverage.partial` carries a reason per kind; `ScheduleGapReason.missingEvents` is gone; `ScheduleGap` gains `day` and `kinds` | A view can say *why* a time is missing ("not published" versus "ambiguous during the clock change"), and SalahAlerts can tell whether a gap touches a prayer whose alerts are on. Kinds moved from the reason to the gap, so one shape describes every gap. |
| `ScheduleGapReason` is a `String` enum | It's a plain label, and `Codable` for vectors. |
| `TimetableSource.init` doesn't throw; bad rows become invalid days | One typo in a yearly table shouldn't disable the whole table, and coverage is the place to report it. |
| `TimetableSource` stores its `WallTimeResolution`; `rows` and `expectedKinds` are `let` | Resolution is part of how the publisher's data is read. Validated data shouldn't be mutated afterwards. |
| The Jumu'ah iqama rule doesn't fall back to the Dhuhr rule | Jumu'ah's congregation follows the khutbah; borrowing Dhuhr's offset would invent a time. |
| `ScheduleState.unavailable` carries `changesAt` | A view or a refresh plan needs to know when an unavailable state ends. |
| `RefreshPlan` carries each entry's state and its `horizon`; `refreshPlan` throws a typed error and defaults its strategy and policy | The states use a lookahead past the horizon (§4.8); a widget that recomputed them with its own bound could show "unknown" near the end of every timeline. The errors replace undefined behavior for an empty horizon or a limit with no room for the cutoff. |
| `HijriDateFormat.components` returns a `Components` struct | Tuples can't be `Hashable` or `Sendable`-checked. |
| `PrayerNames` adds `abbreviation(of: kind)` and `string(for:)` | Symmetry with `name(of:)`, and a way to read an override or catalog entry directly. |
| `PrayerLocale.resolved` takes a `Locale` too, and respects an explicit numbering system | Apps hold `Locale` values, and a Moroccan app that asks for Latin digits must get them. |
| `PrayerLocation` codes its time zone as an identifier string | Readable JSON for vectors and shared settings. |
| `PrayerSchedule` exposes `source` and `iqama`, and caps a query at 400 civil days | Consumers inspect what a schedule was built from. A cap keeps `.distantFuture` from hanging a widget extension. |
| `Hashable` on availability, state, events and plans | Tests and SwiftUI diffing compare them. |
| The catalog is read as JSON from a copied resource on every platform, or from its compiled tables when Xcode 26 compiles it | §6.1: the compiled-catalog lookup isn't available on Linux and returns keys under SwiftPM before 6.4. |
| `.steps` takes steps of at least a second and caps its window at the query limit | §4.8: finer steps can't change a widget and made the grid run without end; an unbounded window would read the source without bound. |

### 6.1 String Catalog lookup with an explicit locale (spike D4)

| Mechanism | macOS, SwiftPM 6.4 | macOS, SwiftPM 6.2 and 6.3 | Linux 6.2-6.4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| D4: `.process` catalog + `LocalizedStringResource(key, locale:, bundle: .atURL(Bundle.module.bundleURL))` | Works: en, ar, tr, ur, `ar-EG` → ar, `fr` → English fallback | Silently wrong: the catalog isn't compiled, `Bundle.module.localizations == ["en"]`, every lookup returns its key | Doesn't compile: no `LocalizedStringResource` or `String.LocalizationValue`; SwiftPM copies the catalog raw (6.2, 6.3) or drops it with "no rule to process" (6.4) |

Decision: ship `Localizable.xcstrings` with `.copy` and read it as JSON everywhere. Translators still edit the catalog in Xcode or as JSON; `needs_review` still marks drafted entries. The same reader runs in app processes and, for SalahWidgets, in extensions. MosqueBoard verifies it in a running app on a simulator (Arabic names chosen in-app while the process language is English).

Xcode 26 doesn't honor `.copy` for a String Catalog when it builds the package inside an Xcode project: it compiles the catalog into `<language>.lproj/Localizable.strings` tables, and the bundle has no `Localizable.xcstrings`. Xcode 27 copies it. MosqueBoard's journeys on `macos-26` (Xcode 26.6, iOS 26.2) caught the English fallback in [run 38427524235](https://github.com/salah-kit/SalahKit/actions/runs/38427524235). So the reader falls back to those tables when the copied file is missing; they keep every value, including `needs_review` ones, but not comments or states. A unit test checks that the tables, as XML and binary property lists, give the same lookups as the JSON.

### 6.2 Linux

On the same runs, Foundation on Linux provided `Duration.UnitsFormatStyle`, `Duration.TimeFormatStyle`, the Islamic Umm al-Qura calendar with `DateFormatter`, `TimeZone.nextDaylightSavingTimeTransition`, `Locale.Components` numbering systems and `Bundle.module` for copied resources. ### 6.3 Default isolation

A probe type declared `nonisolated public struct …: Codable, Sendable` behind a `nonisolated public protocol` built and passed its tests with `-default-isolation MainActor` on Linux 6.4, decoding off the main actor. CI builds SalahKit's targets, tests included, both ways: `swift build --build-tests`, and the same with `SALAHKIT_DEFAULT_ISOLATION=MainActor`, which makes the manifest apply `.defaultIsolation(MainActor.self)` to SalahKit's own targets. It builds MosqueBoard with `SWIFT_DEFAULT_ACTOR_ISOLATION` set to `MainActor` and to `nonisolated`.

Until SalahCalculation (RFC 0002), CI passed `-Xswiftc -default-isolation -Xswiftc MainActor`, which applies to every target in the build, dependencies included. With SalahTimes in the graph that makes SalahTimes' types main-actor-isolated, and every nonisolated caller fails to compile (probe, 2026-10-13: SalahTimes 0.1.0 itself compiles; a nonisolated function using `CalculationParameters` or `PrayerTimes` doesn't). An app's isolation setting never reaches a package's targets, so the per-target switch tests what adopters see. SalahTimes keeps nonisolated default isolation on purpose (its example sets MainActor on its own target only), and SalahKit's examples that link it (CalculatedPhone) set their isolation through an app-only build setting.

### 6.4 Hosted toolchains

Hosted macOS runners offer Xcode up to 26.6 (Swift 6.3.3) on this date; Xcode 27 isn't available there. CI therefore builds with the newest Xcode 26 on `macos-26`, and examples must build with it.

## 8. MosqueBoard data (Amendment 8, §16.1 Q1)

**Search.** On 2026-10-13 we looked for a published timetable whose reuse terms are explicit and verifiable: ministry and open-data portals (data.gov.sg, data.gov.my, data.gov.uk, Brunei), UK, Bosnian and Nordic mosques and councils, and community APIs. UK, Bosnian and Nordic timetables state either no licence or only a copyright notice. Malaysia's 2021 JAKIM table is listed as Creative Commons Attribution, but the archive couldn't be reached to verify it. Computed APIs aren't published timetables.

**Chosen real source.** [Muslim Prayer Timetable 2026](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_d441e7242e78efc566024dd5b0d9829c/view), published by MUIS (Majlis Ugama Islam Singapura) on data.gov.sg, "Free forever for personal or commercial use, under the Open Data Licence" ([Singapore Open Data Licence v1.0](https://data.gov.sg/open-data-licence): "You can use, access, download, copy, distribute, transmit, modify and adapt the datasets … whether commercially or non-commercially", with a conspicuous attribution notice and no suggestion of official status). Both pages were read on 2026-10-13.

How MosqueBoard reads it:
- One row per date, 2026-01-01 to 2026-12-31. Columns Subuh, Syuruk, Zohor, Asar, Maghrib, Isyak map to fajr, sunrise, dhuhr, asr, maghrib, isha. `expectedKinds` is all six.
- Times are local wall-clock times in Singapore (`Asia/Singapore`, UTC+8, no daylight saving). The 2026 rows use 24-hour `HH:mm`, although the column legend says `HH:mm:ss`. Earlier years use a 12-hour clock without AM/PM and aren't used.
- The weekday column is inconsistent ("Thurs", "Tues") and is ignored; `CivilDay.weekday` is used instead.
- The table has no iqama and no Jumu'ah. MosqueBoard's own mosque settings supply them, as a mosque would: `IqamaRules` for daily congregations, and a declared `.jumuah` observance on Fridays with its own iqama rule. This is app policy, visibly separate from the published data.
- The table ends on 2026-12-31, which gives a real expired-coverage case after Isha that evening.

**Synthetic timetable.** No source with clear terms covers daylight saving, events after midnight, published iqama or partial rows. MosqueBoard therefore also bundles a clearly labelled synthetic timetable for a fictional mosque in Edinburgh (`Europe/London`, 55.95° N). It imitates how UK mosque timetables are printed (separate begin and jama'ah times, Jumu'ah at a fixed time that changes with British Summer Time, summer Isha after midnight) without copying any timetable's data. Its begin times are computed (Muslim World League angles, Shafi'i Asr, middle-of-the-night Isha and seventh-of-the-night Fajr at high latitude), and `Examples/MosqueBoard/Timetables.md` records the method and the jama'ah conventions. It runs from 2026-03-01 to 2026-10-31 and covers the spring-forward (03-29) and fall-back (10-25) days, 99 summer nights (05-02 to 08-10) with Isha after midnight (`"+1"`) and four more where only the jama'ah falls after midnight, declared Jumu'ah with its own iqama on every Friday, a partial row (06-24, no Isha), a missing day (06-26, a Friday) and a last published day (10-31). Where real timetables print a capped or fixed summer Isha, this one follows the computed time, which makes the after-midnight case common rather than rare.

**For a person to confirm** (a Needs-you item in A1's status): the MUIS table's Asr convention (presumably Shafi'i) and its meaning around midnight; whether bundling SODL data in this MIT repository with the NOTICE entry is acceptable; and, for the synthetic table, how real Scottish timetables print a time after midnight.

## 9. Open questions

1. **Several Jumu'ah congregations.** Many mosques hold two or three Jumu'ah prayers. The model has one Dhuhr event with one iqama. Should observance carry a list of congregation times? Waiting for a consumer that needs it.
2. **Arabic digits by region.** `PrayerLocale.resolved` gives `arab` digits to every Arabic locale without an explicit numbering system, including Maghreb regions whose CLDR default is Latin. Native-speaker review should decide whether to follow CLDR's regional defaults.
3. **Cross-day overlap.** A high-latitude rule can make Isha fall a minute after the next Fajr. The schedule reports the overlap as an invalid-data gap (§4.6). SalahCalculation reports such kinds as missing instead (RFC 0002 §4.2 step 3), so a calculated schedule shows them as `calculationUnavailable`; a timetable that contradicts itself still gets the invalid-data gap. Either way the overlap is never silently reordered.
4. **Iqama diagnostics.** A discarded iqama (earlier than its prayer) isn't reported anywhere. A validation report API may follow if MosqueBoard or an adopter needs it.
5. **Clocks that went back across midnight.** Some zones once moved their clocks back at 00:01: America/Goose_Bay and America/Moncton until 2011, America/St_Johns until 2010, Antarctica/Casey in 2010. In the repeated hour, an instant after `start(D+1)` still has the Gregorian local date `D`, so `civil(x)` (§4.1) and the day intervals disagree for that hour, and a query whose end falls in it doesn't read day `D+1`. Only historical dates are affected and no vector covers them. If a source ever needs them, `civil(x)` could be defined by the day intervals instead.
6. **Declared observances for a timetable that publishes none.** MUIS publishes no Jumu'ah, so MosqueBoard wraps its `TimetableSource` in a 20-line `PrayerSource` that declares `.jumuah` on every Friday's Dhuhr, labelled as the app's policy. An option on `TimetableSource` would remove the wrapper, but it would make a weekday rule look like published data. Waiting for a second consumer.
7. **Statuses while the state is unavailable.** `.unavailable` carries coverage only, so during a gap a board can't mark the day's earlier prayers as passed; MosqueBoard shows "Unknown". An app can compare event times itself. Whether the state should carry the known past events is open. CalculatedPhone met the other side of it: when a gap hides `nextPrayer`, naming the gap means filtering `coverage.gaps` for prayers.
8. **Expired coverage reads as one gap per day.** Over a two-day lookahead, an expired timetable gives a gap per day, each listing every kind. A summary such as "published data ends at X" may belong in `ScheduleCoverage`; MosqueBoard lists the gaps for now. Coordinates a calculation rejects read the same way in CalculatedPhone: one invalid-data gap per context day, each with all six kinds, for one cause.
9. **Helpers adopters asked for.** SalahFixtures and MosqueBoard asked for: a public wall-time resolver (the instant a clock reads HH:MM on a day in a zone); public initializers for `ScheduleGap` and `ScheduleCoverage`, so tests can compare whole values; a standard lookahead (`start(civil(t) + 2)`, which SalahFixtures' moments use; refresh plans apply the same rule to the horizon's end); and string literals for `WallClockTime` and `CivilDay`. CalculatedPhone added a public order for `ScheduleGapReason`: it sorts gaps by copying §4.6's declaration order. None is in 0.1: each has one consumer so far, MosqueBoard chose a different lookahead, and literals would have to trap on bad text.

10. **Two statuses at one instant.** In Kazan under the middle-of-the-night rule, 20 June's Isha and 21 June's Fajr fall at the same instant, 23:45. At that instant `status(of:)` gives the Isha `.passed` and the Fajr `.justStarted`, by the events order of §4.7. That follows the contract, but a reader may expect the Isha to count as started too. CalculatedPhone shows the contract's answer.

## 10. Changes after the vector review

1. **`PrayerSource.maximumLead`** (2026-10-13, session B1, from the calculated consumer). SalahTimes puts a Fajr bounded by the middle of the night before midnight wherever the clock runs behind the Sun: on 96 days of 2026 in Kazan and Naberezhnye Chelny, 45 in Oskemen with the Muslim World League's angles, 19 to 31 in Jiamusi with the default rule (RFC 0002 §4.4). Under the old rule 4 a calculated source could only report that Fajr missing, which would leave the state unknown for hours on those nights, and the missing Fajr's interval would start at midnight, after the real Fajr. Rules 2 and 4, the context of §4.6 and the missing-event windows now honor a lead the source declares, capped at 12 hours. The requirement has no default, so a wrapper can't drop its base source's lead by accident. `TimetableSource` declares zero, which leaves every timetable answer, every reviewed vector and MosqueBoard unchanged; `LeadTests` covers a source with a lead, and `Vectors/calculation/` covers it end to end.
2. **Rule 6 covers every later day** (2026-10-13, session B1, from review). With a lead, chronology between consecutive days no longer implies it between `D` and `D+2`: when `D+1` has no events, `D`'s Isha late on `D+1` can follow `D+2`'s early Fajr, and the schedule listed them out of order with no gap. Rule 6 now forbids an event after an event of any later day, and the overlap gaps of §4.6 compare each day with the next one that has events. Without a lead nothing changes, so every reviewed vector stands; `LeadTests` covers the crossing, and `Vectors/calculation/ordering.json` covers how `CalculationSource` avoids it.
